## The confessions of an AI programmer

I always loved programming and fascinated doing it - yes I fascinated sitting for long hours in front of a screen writing each and every line of code by myself. I did it for sometime (until the end of 2021 or early 2022) but since it takes time and effort to get good at it and I liked reading papers and theory - I started using AI. I might be one of the very first mainstream adopters of AI for coding, sounds sophisticated but it was nothing but - copy pasting errors into the chatgpt window and pasting back the code again and reading the whole code was a chore that I did at once and that too right before it was going to get shipped to someone else. But I still fascinated the fact of coding everything myself and I even tried to switch to manual coding a few times but it was just too great of an effort after seeing that chatgpt made it work anyways and it was getting better model by model and that in a few years no one would need to code.

And now its 2026 and I think I wasn't completely wrong about it. But I just misjudged the fact that I fascinated seeing those people code manually in vim while all along it was a fascination with their intelligence - the speed at which they would think and type it out through a keyboard. It's been 4 years of burning trillions of dollars from GPT-3.5 to Fable and the people that I once looked up to are doing better than ever before.

## The Dunning-Kruger Effect

Here's how wikipedia explains this effect - "The Dunning-Kruger effect is a cognitive bias where people with limited knowledge or competence in a specific domain greatly overestimate their own skills. It occurs because those lacking expertise also lack the metacognitive ability to recognize their own mistakes or accurately evaluate their true performance."

You might have heard this news a while ago that OpenAI were taking down their model for a while because the model was showing very high sycophancy, which basically means they were being too much of a "yes-man" and praising users for their inputs for no reason at all. We all know why it happened - they fucked up their post-training RL and were not able to keep it undetectable and had to publicly confess. But can we blame them - no we can't because they are just trying to make their business more profitable. Imagine you typed something on chatgpt and it said - "Oh hell no this would never work are you dumb?" and you would just jump on claude and since its a huge sycophant it will be like - "This sounds like a great idea, do you want me to make an MVP for this?" and you will be like oh yes go on and you will be hit by the Dunning-Kruger effect unknowingly, thinking of yourself as a genius - a genius until you run out of tokens but you were not a genius all along.

Nothing of this sort has ever existed before - not just in terms of its capabilities but in terms of addictiveness. Have you ever heard of an addictive technology before, something which makes you feel genius and powerful while working and spending zero focused learning hours.
The psychosis and the hype is real and all around and its completely up to you to recognise what's useful and what's not.
